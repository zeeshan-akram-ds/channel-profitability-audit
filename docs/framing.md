# Framing

ROAS is the default health check most founders use for marketing spend. Good ROAS, keep the channel. Bad ROAS, cut it. That's the whole decision most of the time.

Problem is ROAS only compares revenue to spend. It says nothing about whether the customers a channel brings in ever pay back what it cost to acquire them, or how much margin is actually left once that cost is accounted for properly.

This project tests one specific thing: does a channel's ROAS ranking match its true profitability ranking, once profitability is measured as contribution margin after CAC, tracked over a defined payback window (3 and 6 months). Rank channels by ROAS. Rank them again by contribution margin after CAC within that window. Compare the two.

The finding that matters is whether those rankings disagree. Whether a channel that looks strong on ROAS is actually losing money for months before it pays back, if it ever does. Or the reverse, a channel that gets written off on ROAS turning out to be the most profitable one once payback catches up.

Scope is acquisition channels only, whatever the dataset actually tracks at that level. Exact list gets locked once the data source is chosen, not before.

If the two rankings mostly agree, that's still a real result. It means ROAS holds up fine as a proxy for this dataset, and the more interesting question becomes why it holds here when the general complaint about ROAS is that it usually doesn't.

One thing this project isn't trying to prove: what founders generally know or don't know about their own numbers. That's a claim about people, not something a dataset can settle. Keeping it out of the technical scope entirely.
